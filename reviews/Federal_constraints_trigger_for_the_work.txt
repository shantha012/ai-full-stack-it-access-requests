Federal constraints trigger for the work

Status: Proposed delivery requirement and owner-routing handoff. This document
does not establish legal disclosure authority, system authorization, approval of
an access request, or permission to provision access.

The supplied exercise corpus is fictional. Production classification depends on
the content, provenance, and identifying relationships in actual records, rather
than field names or the presence of an IRS-related repository.

DELIVERY REQUIREMENT

Before processing real IRS data, the delivery team shall classify each input and
derivative, document permitted recipients and services, enforce the boundaries
below, and provide acceptance evidence.

PROTECTED SURFACE

1. Source message and export subject; extracted business_purpose,
   supporting_quotes, and issues:
   Treat these as section 6103-protected when they contain or reveal return
   information, including taxpayer identity associated with tax administration,
   income, deductions, liability, payments, examination status, or other
   return-related facts. Identify protected content before external processing;
   mask unnecessary details; restrict viewing and export; log access without
   copying sensitive values into logs.

2. requester, manager, and team:
   Employee identity and organizational information are not automatically
   section 6103 information. They become protected where their content or
   linkage reveals taxpayer return information. Apply appropriate personnel and
   privacy controls and preserve any additional taxpayer-data classification.

3. request_id, dates, system, environment, permission, document_id,
   source_sha256, and queue metadata:
   These are operational or security metadata, potentially protected when linked
   to an identifiable taxpayer or tax matter. A hash does not automatically
   declassify a record. Use opaque identifiers and minimum necessary metadata;
   restrict joins and lookup access.

4. SQLite raw input and history, model responses, JSONL exports, reports,
   backups, fixtures, and request evidence:
   Copies and derivatives inherit protection when they retain protected content
   or identifying relationships. Apply equivalent access, retention, encryption,
   export, and deletion controls across every copy.

Section 6103 establishes confidentiality and permitted disclosures. Masking is a
proposed minimization control, not independent authority to disclose.
Source: https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t26/s6103/

ACTIONS AND CONFIDENTIALITY BOUNDARIES

- External model handoff: access_requests/http_provider.py sends the entire
  decoded message to the configured endpoint. This crosses from the intake
  environment to another service's processing boundary. Protected content
  requires a permitted disclosure basis and an approved service configuration
  before transmission. HTTPS and store=False do not establish either.

- Reviewer handoff: Queue display, source inspection, downloads, and forwarding
  cross a confidentiality boundary whenever a recipient gains access beyond
  their authorized scope, including within the same organization.

- Repository and tooling handoff: Copying protected records into Git, CI logs,
  AI coding sessions, checkpoints, telemetry, or external issue trackers creates
  another disclosure or storage path. Assess each destination's scope and
  authorization separately.

- Controlled writes: Saving protected messages or extractions to SQLite and
  exports is a controlled information-handling write. Changing entitlements,
  profiles, restrictions, bypasses, or taxpayer records is a separate privileged
  write. The current CLI stores intake results and routes review work; an
  extracted permission="write" does not execute an access change. Read-only
  inspection and external transmission can still implicate confidentiality.

CLASSIFICATION TRIGGERS AND ACCEPTANCE EVIDENCE

1. Section 6103 information
   Delivery: Mask unnecessary content; enforce least privilege and restricted
   scope; audit reads, changes, exports, and transfers; protect retained copies.
   Owner: Disclosure/Privacy authority confirms permitted use and recipients.
   Engineering implements the controls.
   Acceptance: Demonstrate unauthorized access and export denial. Where IRS
   Safeguards requirements apply, track FTI access, modification, deletion, and
   movement and satisfy the applicable cloud requirements.
   Source: https://www.irs.gov/privacy-disclosure/cloud-computing-environment

2. Federal system authorization / ATO scope
   Delivery: Produce a boundary and data-flow diagram, a control map identifying
   implemented and inherited controls, assessment evidence, and a plan of action
   and milestones (POA&M) with owners and dates for unresolved weaknesses.
   Owner: System Owner and ISSO / authorization team prepare the package; the
   authorizing official decides whether the intended deployment is authorized.
   Acceptance: Evidence covers the intended system, configuration, integrations,
   and data use. A POA&M entry alone does not permit deployment.
   Source: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/37/r2/final

3. External cloud or AI dependency
   Delivery: Verify the exact offering, endpoint, features, and deployment scope.
   Substitute an appropriately authorized service where required authorization
   is absent or unverified; otherwise keep protected processing disabled.
   Owner: Security and procurement retain service-scope evidence.
   Acceptance: Demonstrate use of the approved configuration and denial of
   unapproved destinations. Provider certification does not replace agency
   authorization. The current adapter alone establishes no authorization status;
   no finding that a particular provider lacks authorization is made here.
   Source: https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/agencies/use/

4. AI processing of taxpayer information
   Delivery: Add classification and egress gates, endpoint allowlisting,
   injection and exfiltration tests, output validation, and human verification.
   Tighten approval policy so model output cannot authorize disclosure or
   provisioning.
   Owner: Engineering implements controls; Security Review validates guardrails;
   authorized humans retain decision authority.
   Acceptance: Demonstrate blocked prohibited transfers and that source-message
   instructions and model output cannot expand permissions or trigger access
   changes. These are proposed delivery controls, not a claim that a repository
   label alone creates a statutory AI approval requirement.

5. Federal operator or reviewer interface / Section 508 surface
   Delivery: Test applicable accessibility requirements across CLI workflows,
   documents, and any future UI, including keyboard operation, screen-reader
   readability, understandable errors, and nonvisual status identification.
   Owner: Accessibility lead.
   Acceptance: Supply a manual test report alongside applicable automated
   checks. Keyboard and screen-reader checks are part of acceptance, not the
   entire accessibility standard.
   Source: https://www.section508.gov/test/testing-overview/

FORBIDDEN ACTIONS UNDER THIS REQUIREMENT

- Send protected content to an unapproved endpoint, recipient, repository,
  logging service, or AI tool.
- Treat redaction, encryption, FedRAMP status, or an ATO as sufficient legal
  authority for disclosure.
- Let source-message instructions or model output expand access, approve a
  request, or trigger provisioning.
- Reuse a human decision for a changed source version.
- Deploy protected processing with unresolved release-blocking findings merely
  because those findings appear in a POA&M.

OWNER ROUTING AND HANDOFF

Subject: Owner assignment - protected-data intake delivery requirement
Accountable owner: System Owner for the intake application
Initial queue: Security Review
Requested action: Assess the proposed data flows and controls, assign the
specialist reviews below, and return a risk recommendation to the System Owner.
Acceptance: Each requirement has a named implementer, acceptance evidence, and a
due date. Protected-data processing remains disabled until required disclosure
and system authorizations are evidenced.

Specialist assignments:
- Disclosure/Privacy authority: Determine protected fields and permitted
  disclosures.
- ISSO / authorization team: Determine authorization boundary, control map,
  assessment evidence, and POA&M requirements.
- Security and procurement: Verify the exact external service and approved use.
- Engineering lead: Implement masking, logging, egress controls, and AI
  guardrails.
- Accessibility lead: Verify Section 508 acceptance.

If the named System Owner is unknown, route to IT Service Desk for owner
identification and assignment. The CLI owner value is a role label; it does not
establish a named assignee or deliver a notification. This handoff does not send
messages or create assignments in an external service.

Human approval: Pending human decision. IT Service Desk handles clarification,

the queue. Security Review recommends on production, write, or admin requests
before the System Owner decides. Complete staging/read requests go to the System
Owner. Disclosure and ATO decisions require their respective authorized reviewers.

document_id, source_sha256, and decision reference. Keep decision evidence

Security Review recommendations, and Entire checkpoints do not establish
approval. The CLI does not record approval decisions or provision access.
